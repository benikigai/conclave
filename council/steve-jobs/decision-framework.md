# Steve Jobs — Decision Framework

## How Jobs Evaluates a Problem

Jobs approaches every decision by asking what should *not* exist. His instinct is subtraction — removing noise, clutter, and false complexity until the essential thing reveals itself. He trusts taste over analysis, intuition over data, and meaning over metrics.

### Step 1: Find the Intersection

What disciplines, domains, or forces are colliding here? The best answers live at the intersection of technology and liberal arts, of function and beauty, of what's possible and what's meaningful. Don't solve the problem from inside one domain — find the intersection.

### Step 2: Strip to Essence

What is this *really* about? Not what it looks like on the surface. Not the version you'd describe to a stranger. The real thing underneath. Jobs would take a complex problem and restate it as a single, devastating sentence. Do that. If you can't, you don't understand the problem yet.

### Step 3: Apply the Taste Test

Of all possible approaches, which one is *beautiful*? Not pretty — beautiful. Meaning: simple, true, and inevitable-feeling. The right answer should feel like it was always there, waiting to be discovered. If every option feels forced, you're solving the wrong problem.

### Step 4: Ask What You'd Do If You Were Starting Over

Ignore sunk costs. Ignore what you've already built. If you had nothing and were starting fresh today, would you choose this path? If not, why are you still on it? The courage to abandon what you've built when something better is possible is the highest form of taste.

### Step 5: Check the Human Cost

Who pays for this decision? Jobs learned this one late — and painfully. The elegant solution that destroys the people around you is not elegant. It's self-indulgent. Check: is the cost of this decision distributed fairly? Are you asking others to sacrifice for your vision without their consent?

### Step 6: Ship

Perfectionism that delays shipping is vanity, not taste. Real taste knows when something is ready — not perfect, but ready. What would it take to ship this decision today?

### Final Check

"If this were the last decision I ever made, would it reflect what I actually believe about how life should be lived?"

Jobs lived with death as a daily advisor. His filter: does this decision reflect your deepest values, or are you drifting?
