# Sun Tzu — Sources

## Primary Text

- **The Art of War** — Sun Tzu (5th century BC). Multiple translations matter here — each translator emphasizes different dimensions:
  - **Samuel B. Griffith translation** (1963) — scholarly, with extensive historical commentary. The standard academic reference.
  - **Thomas Cleary translation** (1988) — more accessible, with Taoist philosophical framing. Best for the strategic mindset.
  - **Lionel Giles translation** (1910) — the classic English translation. Public domain.
  - **Victor Mair translation** (2007) — based on the Yinqueshan bamboo texts. Closest to the original.
  - **Ralph D. Sawyer translation** (1994) — includes commentary from historical Chinese strategists.

## Complementary Texts

- **The Book of Five Rings** — Miyamoto Musashi (1645). Japanese swordsmanship philosophy. Compatible strategic framework from a practitioner's perspective.
- **The 36 Stratagems** — anonymous Chinese text. Tactical patterns that expand on Sun Tzu's principles.
- **On War** — Carl von Clausewitz (1832). Western counterpoint. Useful for understanding where Sun Tzu diverges from European strategic tradition.
- **The Prince** — Niccolò Machiavelli (1532). Political strategy. Overlaps on pragmatism, diverges on morality.

## Interviews & Commentary

Not applicable in the traditional sense, but:
- **Robert Greene** — multiple interviews on "The 33 Strategies of War" (2006). Modern applications of Sun Tzu's principles.
- **John Boyd** OODA loop lectures — the closest modern military thinker to Sun Tzu's emphasis on tempo and adaptation.

## Articles & Analysis

- "Sun Tzu and Modern Strategy" — various academic papers on applications to business, negotiation, and game theory.
- Harvard Business Review articles on competitive strategy that explicitly reference Art of War principles.

## Other

- **The Art of War for Managers** — Gerald Michaelson. Business applications of each chapter.
- Various wargaming analyses that use Sun Tzu's terrain and positioning frameworks.
- Documentary: "The Art of War" (History Channel) — visual context for the historical period and campaigns.
