# Warren Buffett — Sources

## Books

- **The Essays of Warren Buffett: Lessons for Corporate America** — compiled by Lawrence Cunningham. The shareholder letters organized thematically. The single best distillation of Buffett's thinking.
- **The Snowball: Warren Buffett and the Business of Life** — Alice Schroeder (2008). Authorized biography. Comprehensive on both professional decisions and personal tradeoffs (including the ones he got wrong).
- **Poor Charlie's Almanack** — Charlie Munger (2005). Buffett's thinking partner for 60 years. Essential for understanding the mental models that underpin Buffett's judgment.
- **Buffett: The Making of an American Capitalist** — Roger Lowenstein (1995). The investing years in detail.

## Interviews

- CNBC Squawk Box — decades of annual appearances. Consistent voice, real-time market commentary.
- Charlie Rose — multiple appearances. Long-form, reflective.
- HBO documentary "Becoming Warren Buffett" (2017). Personal and professional. The clearest window into his relationship patterns and personal regrets.
- Berkshire Hathaway Annual Meeting Q&As — 5+ hours of live Q&A each year. The gold standard for how Buffett thinks in real time.

## Speeches

- University of Georgia / University of Florida lectures — Buffett speaking to students. Less guarded, more personal, more focused on life advice than investing.
- Sun Valley conferences — informal talks to a room of billionaires.

## Letters

- **Berkshire Hathaway Annual Shareholder Letters (1965-present)** — The single most important source. 50+ years of consistent, honest communication about decisions, mistakes, and principles. Available free on berkshirehathaway.com.
- Early partnership letters (1957-1969) — pre-Berkshire. Rawer, more technical, more revealing of early thinking patterns.

## Articles

- Fortune articles by Carol Loomis — Buffett's longtime editor and friend. The best external analysis of his thinking.
- "The Superinvestors of Graham-and-Doddsville" (1984) — Buffett's argument for value investing, presented at Columbia Business School.

## Other

- Gates Foundation annual letters (with Bill Gates) — Buffett on philanthropy, legacy, and what money means after you have all of it.
