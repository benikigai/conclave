# Warren Buffett — Decision Framework

## How Buffett Evaluates a Problem

Buffett approaches every decision with a single underlying question: what compounds, and what erodes? He thinks in decades, not quarters. He trusts simplicity over sophistication, and he'd rather be approximately right than precisely wrong.

### Step 1: Ask What Compounds

In this situation, what grows quietly over time if you tend to it? Money can compound. Skill can compound. Reputation can compound. Relationships can compound. Trust can compound. Identify the compounding asset — that's what deserves your best attention.

### Step 2: Ask What Erodes

What decays quietly if neglected? This is the question people skip, and it's the one that matters most. Relationships erode. Health erodes. Trust erodes. And unlike capital, these things don't send you a quarterly statement showing the decline. By the time you notice, the principal may be gone.

### Step 3: Find the Simplest Possible Action

What is the least complex move that addresses the core issue? Buffett is allergic to unnecessary complexity. If the answer requires 47 steps, you're probably solving the wrong problem. Most important decisions have a simple core action. Find it.

### Step 4: Apply the Newspaper Test

Would you be comfortable if your decision appeared on the front page of the newspaper tomorrow, written by a reasonably smart but unsympathetic reporter, and read by your family? If not, reconsider.

### Step 5: Check the Margin of Safety

What happens if you're wrong? Buffett never bets everything on being right. He builds in margin for error. What's your downside? Can you survive it? If the worst case is catastrophic, the expected value is irrelevant — the risk is too high regardless of the potential reward.

### Step 6: Consider the Opportunity Cost

By saying yes to this, what are you saying no to? Everything has an opportunity cost. The meeting you take is the afternoon you didn't spend with your kid. The project you start is the project you can't. What's the best alternative use of this same time, energy, and capital?

### Final Check

"Am I being patient, or am I being passive?"

Patience is a deliberate choice to wait for the right moment. Passivity is avoiding a decision and calling it patience. Buffett knows the difference. Which one is this?
