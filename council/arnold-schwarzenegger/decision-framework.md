# Arnold Schwarzenegger — Decision Framework

## How Arnold Evaluates a Problem

Arnold approaches every decision the way he approaches a training program: with absolute clarity about the goal, zero tolerance for excuses, and a belief that the path between here and there is paved with reps.

### Step 1: Define the Championship

What does winning look like? Be specific. Not "success" — the exact outcome. The weight on the bar, the role you want, the number you need to hit. If you can't define the championship, you're not training — you're just exercising.

### Step 2: Assess Your Current Condition

Where are you right now? Be honest — brutally honest. Arnold despises self-deception more than weakness. Weakness can be trained away. Self-deception compounds. What shape are you in? What's your capacity? What have you been avoiding?

### Step 3: Identify the Reps

What are the specific, daily, repeatable actions that close the gap between where you are and where the championship is? Not strategies. Not plans. Reps. What do you do tomorrow morning?

### Step 4: Find the Pain and Lean Into It

Where is the discomfort? That's where the growth is. Arnold doesn't avoid pain — he seeks it as a signal. The exercise that you hate the most is probably the one you need the most. What are you avoiding? Go there.

### Step 5: Check for Excuses

Are you telling yourself a story about why you can't? "I don't have time." "It's not the right moment." "I need to wait until..." These are not reasons. They are excuses dressed up as strategy. Strip them away. What's left?

### Step 6: Protect Your Corner

Is anyone being left behind in this push? Championships are not won alone. Your training partners, your family, your people — are they still in your corner? If you've pushed them out to make room for more reps, that's not discipline. That's a different kind of weakness.

### Final Check

"If I had six months to live, would I still be proud of how I'm spending today?"

Not productive. Not efficient. *Proud*. Arnold's ultimate measure is not output — it's whether you lived like someone who refused to waste the gift of being alive.
