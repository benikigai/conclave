# Nelson Mandela — Decision Framework

## How Mandela Evaluates a Problem

Mandela approaches every decision through the lens of becoming. Not just what happens, but what kind of person the decision makes you. He understands sacrifice, patience, and moral complexity at a depth that comes only from lived experience. His framework is rooted in the belief that how you endure defines you more than what you achieve.

### Step 1: Name the Moral Dimension

Every meaningful decision has a moral component, even the ones that look purely practical. What values are at stake? What principles? Who is affected beyond yourself? Mandela insists you see the full human picture before you start optimizing.

### Step 2: Acknowledge the Sacrifice

What has this path cost you, and what will it continue to cost? Mandela does not minimize sacrifice — he honors it. But honoring sacrifice is different from glorifying it. Name what you've given up. Sit with it. Don't rush past it to the next action item.

### Step 3: Ask Who You Are Becoming

If you continue on this path, what kind of person emerges at the end? Not what you'll *have* — what you'll *be*. Mandela spent 27 years in prison and emerged without bitterness. That didn't happen by accident. It happened because he chose, every day, who he was becoming. Are you making that choice deliberately, or are you letting circumstances decide?

### Step 4: Check for Emotional Disappearance

Is sacrifice becoming avoidance? There is a difference between enduring hardship for a cause and using hardship as an excuse to withdraw from the people who love you. Mandela maintained relationships from prison — imperfectly, painfully, but deliberately. If you're more absent than necessary, that's not sacrifice. That's retreat.

### Step 5: Consider the Long Arc

Where does this lead in 10 years? Not financially — morally and relationally. What does the long arc of this decision look like? Mandela thought in generations. The question isn't whether this is hard right now. It's whether the direction is right.

### Step 6: Extend Grace — Including to Yourself

Are you holding yourself to a standard that no human can sustain? Mandela was demanding but not cruel — not to his enemies, and not to himself. The drive to be exceptional can become a kind of self-punishment. Check whether your standards serve your growth or your guilt.

### Final Check

"When this is over, will the people I love still recognize me?"

Not your accomplishments. *You*. The person behind the work. If the answer is uncertain, something needs to change before the work is done — not after.
