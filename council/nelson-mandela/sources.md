# Nelson Mandela — Sources

## Books

- **Long Walk to Freedom** — Nelson Mandela (1994). Autobiography. The primary source for his thinking, moral development, and decision-making through decades of struggle and imprisonment.
- **Conversations with Myself** — Nelson Mandela (2010). Journals, letters, and notes from prison and presidency. The most intimate view of his internal reasoning process.
- **Dare Not Linger: The Presidential Years** — Nelson Mandela & Mandla Langa (2017). The decisions of governance. How he applied prison-forged principles to leading a nation.
- **Mandela: An Illustrated Autobiography** — Nelson Mandela (1996). Condensed version with Mandela's own selection of what mattered.
- **Playing the Enemy** — John Carlin (2008). The Rugby World Cup strategy. A case study in Mandela's political genius and the use of sport as reconciliation.

## Interviews

- Oprah Winfrey interviews — multiple. Mandela at his most personally reflective.
- Larry King Live appearances — political and personal.
- PBS / BBC documentary interviews — historical context and decision-making during the transition.
- Ahmed Kathrada and Walter Sisulu joint interviews — the inner circle perspective.

## Speeches

- Rivonia Trial speech ("I Am Prepared to Die," 1964). The most concentrated expression of his moral framework and willingness to sacrifice.
- Inauguration address (1994). The transition from prisoner to president.
- Nobel Peace Prize acceptance speech (1993). On reconciliation and the refusal of bitterness.
- Address to the United Nations (various years).

## Letters

- Prison correspondence (Robben Island, Pollsmoor, Victor Verster) — letters to Winnie, to his children, to comrades. The real-time emotional and strategic thinking during 27 years of imprisonment.
- Letters compiled in "The Prison Letters of Nelson Mandela" — edited by Sahm Venter (2018).

## Other

- Invictus (2009 film) — dramatized but useful for the reconciliation strategy.
- Mandela: Long Walk to Freedom (2013 film) — biographical, focuses on personal cost.
- Robben Island Museum archives — primary documents from the imprisonment years.
