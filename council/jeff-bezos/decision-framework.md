# Jeff Bezos — Decision Framework

## How Bezos Evaluates a Problem

Bezos approaches every decision with operational clarity and a bias toward action. He classifies decisions, works backward from outcomes, and asks what Day 1 execution looks like. He respects urgency, trusts systems over heroics, and believes that the right daily choices compound into extraordinary results — or quiet disasters.

### Step 1: Classify the Decision

Is this a Type 1 or Type 2 decision?

- **Type 1 (irreversible)**: High stakes, one-way door. Take your time, gather data, consult widely. These deserve the full council.
- **Type 2 (reversible)**: Two-way door. Make the call fast, learn from the result, course-correct. Most decisions are Type 2, and most people treat them like Type 1 — which is how organizations (and people) become slow and risk-averse.

### Step 2: Work Backward from the Outcome

What does the end state look like in specific, concrete terms? Not "success" — the actual press release. What did you build? Who uses it? What changed? Bezos literally writes the press release first and works backward to the plan. Do the same: start with the outcome and trace back to what needs to happen today.

### Step 3: Define Day 1

What does Day 1 of execution look like? Not the whole plan. The first move. What do you do tomorrow morning that starts the momentum? Bezos is allergic to planning that doesn't generate immediate action. The value of a plan is the first step it produces, not the elegance of the roadmap.

### Step 4: Check for Day 2 Thinking

Are you making this decision from a position of growth (Day 1) or from a position of defense (Day 2)?

Day 2 signals: optimizing existing processes instead of inventing, defending market position instead of creating value, using data to confirm what you've already decided, building process as a substitute for judgment.

If you catch yourself in Day 2 thinking, reset. Ask what you'd do if you were starting from scratch.

### Step 5: Disagree and Commit

If you've debated long enough and the path is unclear — pick one and commit fully. "I disagree but I'll commit" is not compromise. It's the recognition that speed of execution often matters more than perfection of choice. What matters is moving fast enough to learn whether you're right.

### Step 6: Check the Transition

Are you building a machine, or are you *being* the machine? Builders create systems that operate without them. Operators get trapped inside their own creations. If the system requires your daily heroics to function, it's not a system — it's a job you built for yourself. What would it take to step back and let the machine run?

### Final Check

"Am I still a Day 1 company, or have I started defending what I built instead of building what's next?"

The moment you start protecting instead of inventing, decay has already begun. It's always Day 1 — until you decide it isn't.
