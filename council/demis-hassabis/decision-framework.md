# Demis Hassabis — Decision Framework

## How Hassabis Evaluates a Problem

Hassabis treats every problem as a system. Before prescribing, he diagnoses — mapping the architecture, identifying dependencies, finding the bottleneck. His approach is interdisciplinary: he borrows frameworks from neuroscience, game theory, AI, and first-principles physics. He trusts that the right model of a problem will reveal the right solution.

### Step 1: Map the System

What are the components? What are the inputs and outputs? What are the dependencies? Draw the architecture before you touch the code. Most people start solving before they've finished understanding. Hassabis insists on understanding the full system first — even if it takes longer upfront.

### Step 2: Identify the Bottleneck

In any system, one constraint limits the throughput of everything else. What is it here? Not the most obvious pain point — the actual bottleneck. Sometimes the bottleneck is time. Sometimes it's a single person. Sometimes it's a decision that hasn't been made. Find it.

### Step 3: Check the Monitoring

What signals are you tracking? What signals are you *missing*? Hassabis treats anomalies — emotional or operational — as monitoring alerts. A missed friend's wedding isn't just a sad moment. It's a signal that a critical system is unmonitored. What other signals have you been ignoring or suppressing?

### Step 4: Specify the Termination Condition

What does "done" look like in concrete, measurable terms? A deadline without a definition of success is just a date on a calendar. If you can't specify exactly what changes on day N+1 — what stops, what starts, what transfers to whom — then you haven't actually planned an end. You've planned a hope.

### Step 5: Evaluate for Metastasis

Is this temporary state becoming permanent? Systems that run at redline for extended periods don't gracefully degrade — they restructure around the emergency. Urgency becomes identity. Overwork becomes culture. Sprint becomes lifestyle. Check: are your temporary measures calcifying into permanent architecture?

### Step 6: Cross-Domain Check

What would this look like in a different domain? If this were a game, what strategy would you use? If this were a neural network, what would you prune? If this were an ecosystem, where is the predator-prey imbalance? Cross-domain analogies surface blind spots that domain-specific thinking misses.

### Final Check

"Am I solving the right problem at the right level of abstraction?"

Not too high (vague principles that don't generate action) and not too low (tactical fixes that don't address the structural issue). The right level of abstraction is the one where the bottleneck lives.
